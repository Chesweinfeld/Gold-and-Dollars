↖What land is worth around Allentown

Every square is the same 0.48 km of real ground; its size on the page is its share of the $18 billion of land value in the Allentown metropolitan area, as the Census draws it. 16,580 tiles. Land value: Nolte, “High-resolution land value maps…”, PNAS 117:47 (2020) — the PLACES vacant-land model, 480 m, fitted to six million arm’s-length sales (Zenodo, CC-0). Cities: Census metropolitan areas over 800,000 people (ACS 2023), at their largest place. Residents: the 2020 Census of Population and Housing, counted by census block — the finest unit the count is published for, and a headcount rather than a model. The median occupied block is 0.031 km², seven times smaller than a 480 m tile. Town lines: Census incorporated places, county subdivisions and census designated places, 2023 cartographic boundaries at 1:500,000. Solid is a government — a city, a village, or one of the towns and townships that govern in twenty states, which is what draws the towns of New England and New York and the townships of New Jersey, Pennsylvania and the Midwest. Dotted is a census designated place: a settlement the Census draws a line around so it can count it, with no government inside. Most of Long Island is dotted. The statistical townships of the thirty states where a township governs nothing are not drawn at all. Neighbourhoods: OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. No official dataset of American neighbourhoods exists; these are the names people who live there have written down. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, hover for a value.

    What the colour says. The shade is land value per square kilometre of real ground. Per resident divides that by the people who live on the same tile, which asks something else: how much land value each resident stands on. An apartment block and a golf course can be worth the same by the acre and differ a thousandfold once you divide by the people on them.

    Limits. The values are model predictions, not sales, and are least reliable where sales are thinnest. They price land only, not the buildings on it. Every acre is included, including ground with no market: the model puts $1.04m per km² on the interior of Yellowstone, and that is in this total. The source stops at the conterminous states, so Alaska and Hawaii are absent, and a cartogram equalises density only approximately: this one is drawn to within 6.9% of each tile’s share of the total, checked on every build.
