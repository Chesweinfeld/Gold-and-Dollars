↖What land is worth around Milwaukee

A cartogram sizes each place by a number instead of by its area. Every square here is the same 0.48 km of real ground, drawn at its share of the $29 billion of land value in the Milwaukee metropolitan area, as the Census draws it, so expensive land swells and cheap land shrinks to a thread. 16,741 tiles; scroll to zoom, hover for a value.

    What the colour says. The shade is land value per square kilometre of real ground.

    Limits. These are model predictions, not sales, and they are weakest where sales are thinnest. They price the land, not the buildings on it. Every acre counts, including ground no one trades: the model puts $1.04m per km² on the interior of Yellowstone, and that sum is in this total. The source covers the conterminous states only; Alaska and Hawaii are absent. A cartogram equalises density only approximately, and each build checks this one against every tile’s share of the total.

    Sources. Land value: Nolte, “High-resolution land value maps…”, PNAS 117:47 (2020) — the PLACES vacant-land model, 480 m, fitted to six million arm’s-length sales (Zenodo, CC-0). Cities: Census metropolitan areas over 800,000 people (ACS 2023), at their largest place. Town lines: Census incorporated places, county subdivisions and census designated places, 2023 boundaries at 1:500,000. A heavier line is a government — a city, a village, or one of the towns and townships that govern in twenty states, which is what draws New England, New York, and the townships of New Jersey, Pennsylvania and the Midwest. A lighter line is a census designated place: a settlement the Census outlines in order to count it, with no government inside. Most of Long Island is one. Statistical townships, in the thirty states where a township governs nothing, are not drawn. Neighbourhoods: OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). No official dataset of American neighbourhoods exists; these are the names residents have recorded.
