What American land is worth

A map of the lower 48 drawn at price rather than at area. Every square is the same 3.84 km of real ground; its size on the page is its share of the $11.26 trillion the country’s land is worth.

The whole country → 533,958 tiles carrying $11.26 trillion, from Nolte’s 480 m price surface. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, hover for a value.

Closer in, at the source’s native 480 m

New York → $998 billion in a 220 km square. Zoom in and Manhattan swells until the East River is the only thing holding it off Queens. San Francisco → The peninsula and the South Bay, where an acre costs more than anywhere else in the country.

Land value is Nolte, “High-resolution land value maps…”, PNAS 117:47 (2020) — the PLACES vacant-land model, fitted to six million arm’s-length sales, CC-0. Each cartogram is drawn to within 6.9% of every tile’s share of the total, checked on every build. The code that makes them, and its own account of where they are wrong, is at github.com/Chesweinfeld/Gold-and-Dollars.
